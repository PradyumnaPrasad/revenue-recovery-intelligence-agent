# Declared generative model

This is the exact data-generating process implemented in
`app/simulation/generator.py`. Publishing it is what turns the synthetic
portfolio from "numbers we made up" into "an honest, inspectable simulation"
— see plan.md §6.1 and §6.4 (C1: circular evaluation).

Seed determinism: `numpy.random.default_rng(seed)` + `Faker.seed(seed)`.
Same `(size, seed)` -> byte-identical business data, verified by
`portfolio_fingerprint()` and `tests/unit/test_generator.py`.

## Segment and industry mix

```text
segment  ~ Categorical(smb=0.55, mid_market=0.32, enterprise=0.13)
industry ~ Categorical(saas=0.22, manufacturing=0.20, logistics=0.16,
                        healthcare=0.14, retail=0.18, education=0.10)
```

## Customer payment behaviour

```text
late_rate ~ Beta(a(segment), b(segment)), clipped to [0, 0.95]
  smb:        Beta(2.0, 4.0)   (mean ~0.33, wide spread)
  mid_market: Beta(1.6, 5.0)   (mean ~0.24)
  enterprise: Beta(1.2, 7.0)   (mean ~0.15, tighter procurement)

broken_promises ~ Poisson(lambda = 0.3 + 4.5 * late_rate), capped at 8
  # correlation is structural, not post-hoc: a customer with a higher
  # late_rate has a higher Poisson rate for broken promises by construction.

avg_days_to_pay = max(0, 10 + 25 * late_rate + Normal(0, 5))

contact_count_30d ~ min(4, Poisson(0.6 + 1.5 * late_rate))
```

## Invoice

```text
amount_paise ~ LogNormal(mu(segment), sigma(segment)), clipped [Rs 5,000, Rs 50,00,000]
  smb:        LogNormal(10.9, 0.55)   (median ~ Rs 54,000)
  mid_market: LogNormal(12.4, 0.65)   (median ~ Rs 2.4L)
  enterprise: LogNormal(14.2, 0.75)   (median ~ Rs 14.8L)

dispute_flag ~ Bernoulli(p = 0.55 * base(industry) + 0.02 * 1[amount_paise > Rs 5,00,000])
  base(industry): saas=0.04, manufacturing=0.10, logistics=0.07,
                   healthcare=0.12, retail=0.05, education=0.06
  (0.55 scale factor and the 0.05->0.02 high-value bump are a plan.md F4
  correction: unscaled, this gave a measured 7.2% disputed against a real
  B2B rate of 2-5%; scaled, it measures ~4% across seeds.)

days_overdue ~ Gamma(shape = 1.6 + 2.0 * late_rate, scale = scale(segment)),
               clipped [1, 180]
  scale(segment): smb=10.0, mid_market=14.0, enterprise=18.0

payment_link_sent  ~ Bernoulli(0.6)
payment_link_opened ~ Bernoulli(0.55 - 0.3 * late_rate) | payment_link_sent
```

## Promise-to-pay

```text
P(has_promise) = min(0.5, 0.15 + 0.5 * late_rate)
promised_date  = now + Uniform(2, 20) days
kept ~ Bernoulli(max(0.1, 0.75 - 0.12 * broken_promises))
```

Both `kept=True` and `kept=False` outcomes are generated by construction —
required so a downstream model sees both successful and broken promises
(plan.md, original design, preserved).

## Inbound reply corpus

Zero to three replies per invoice, intent sampled straight from the declared
target distribution (`app/simulation/reply_templates.py::TARGET_DISTRIBUTION`)
— no reweighting by `dispute_flag` or any other invoice state.

**Replies are generated but deliberately unread.** `has_open_dispute_reply`
in `InvoiceFacts` is always `False` at generation and training-data time; it
can only become `True` once the M6 extraction layer actually processes a
reply's text. An earlier version of this generator derived it directly from
the pre-written corpus (i.e. treated unread text as already-extracted
evidence) combined with a 4x dispute-reply reweighting, which together
inflated the measured `disputed` diagnosis share to 19.7% of the portfolio —
a fifth of every batch exiting at policy rule P01 before ever reaching a
ranked decision. Both are corrected (plan.md F4).

## What this buys us (plan.md §6.4)

Because every distribution above is stated, not hidden, the honest claim
this project makes is: *given this declared environment, the decision layer
captures Y% of the available uplift over a fixed-cadence baseline* — not
"this agent recovers X% more money in the real world." The three-environment
robustness test (`E_train` / `E_shift` / `E_adversarial`, added in M1)
perturbs these parameters directly and re-measures uplift.
